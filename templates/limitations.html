Known Limitations

Empire Forge builds your mod by editing the game's own data file. A few things the game engine doesn't allow, and a few we haven't built yet — here's what you might run into.

How custom civs fit into the game
  • Each custom civ takes over an existing civ's slot. You choose which one when you build the mod. The original is replaced in the mod only — your game files are never touched, and switching the mod off brings it back.
  • Chronicles civs aren't available — Achaemenids, Athenians, Spartans, Macedonians, Thracians and Puru, along with their team bonuses. They only exist in the Chronicles game mode, so nothing built from them would work in a normal match.
  • Every mod comes in two parts: a data mod (the civs themselves) and a UI mod (all the text on the civ selection screen). If your civs play correctly but their names, bonuses or descriptions look wrong, raise the UI mod's priority in the game's mod menu — even when it's the only mod you have enabled.
Limits set by the game engine

These can't be worked around. Where one applies to your civ, the builder warns you before you build.

  • Very large civs can crash the game at startup. The game refuses to load if any single effect holds more than about 189 commands. Two parts of a civ are one effect each and can grow that large: the tech tree (every node you switch off adds a command) and the team bonus (every team bonus you pick is merged into it). The builder warns you if either gets close. Custom bonuses split themselves automatically, so they never hit this.
  • A building has 24 training buttons — two pages of twelve. When more units share a building than fit, the builder moves the extras to page two; if both pages are full, it tells you which unit couldn't be placed.
  • A unit can cost at most two resources. Units have three cost slots and the third holds population, so a unit can't be made to cost food, wood and gold.
  • Some unique techs are run by the game's own scripts, which name the original civ's units directly. Your civ gets only part of the effect:
    • Coiled Serpent Array — the Spearman half works; the unique-unit half doesn't.
    • Red Cliffs Tactics — the Fire Archer half only works if your civ has Fire Archers.
    • Butalmapu — the discount doesn't reach your own unique unit.
  • "Ignores armor" isn't possible. Units like the Leitis have it built into the game engine itself, so it can't be given to another unit.
  • Some units overlap in the in-game tech tree viewer (F2) when two share a position — for example a ram over a siege elephant. This is cosmetic only; the units train normally.
Civ and team bonuses
  • "Receive +100 food per Town Center" and "New Town Centers spawn 2 sheep" have no multiplier. Both work by having the Town Center spawn a hidden unit, so the amount is fixed in the game's data rather than a number we can scale.
  • Those two bonuses also share one Town Center mechanism. A civ with both will most likely lose the 100 food from the Castle Age on, when the sheep bonus takes over. Pick one.
  • Bonuses we can't build aren't offered. If a bonus you remember from another tool is missing from the picker, it's because it can't be made to work properly — we'd rather leave a card out than ship one that does nothing.
Custom bonuses
  • Villager job cards can't change HP, cost or train time. A villager carries its current HP across job changes, so an HP bonus that only applies while building could behave unpredictably; and only the base Villager is ever trained. Use the All villagers tile for those.
  • Techs can be made cheaper, faster or free — not stronger, yet. Cards like "Forging gives +2 attack" are planned.
  • A flat tech cost change only touches resources the tech already costs, and never takes a cost below zero.
  • "Unique unit" and "Unique techs" cards follow whichever civ uses them. A civ without a unique unit or unique techs skips that card and tells you so.

Found something that isn't listed here? Let us know on the Empire Forge Discord — the link is at the bottom of every page.
